01 · SQL · Customer analytics

Customer Campaign Effectiveness & Segmentation Analysis

Business question: Which customer groups respond to marketing campaigns, how does response vary across campaigns, and where could more targeted customer engagement create value?

Dataset

Customer-level iFood marketing campaign data with 2,205 customer records, covering demographics, household characteristics, purchasing behaviour, channel usage and campaign responses.

Analysis

Used SQL to examine campaign acceptance, customer spending by education and marital-status groups, channel behaviour by household composition, response-group behaviour, age bands and complaint patterns.

Tools

SQL · Excel · Customer analytics

Key findings
Campaign responseCampaign 2 had an acceptance rate of approximately 1.4%, while the latest campaign response variable was approximately 15.1%.
Education & spendThe average total spend was approximately $640 for customers in the PhD group versus approximately $59 for the Basic group.
Marital status & spendThe average total spend was approximately $672 for widowed customers versus approximately $547 for married customers.
Visualisation
Average total spend by education group SQL analysis · visual summary
Illustrative visualisation of the documented SQL finding; values are rounded as reported in the project.
What this suggests

Campaign performance and customer value are not uniform across the customer base. Segment-level analysis can therefore support more targeted experimentation rather than treating all customers identically.

Recommendations: prioritise high-response segments for testing; tailor campaign propositions to behavioural groups; investigate why some campaigns underperform; and track incremental response rather than relying only on overall campaign averages.

02 · Tableau · Data visualisation

Work-related concussion claims in New Zealand

Business question: Which industries and occupations are seeing the fastest growth in work-related concussion claims, and what does that shift mean for where prevention effort should go next?

Analysis

Cleaned and structured six years of claims data in SQL, then built a Tableau dashboard tracking claim volume across five major industries and comparing growth rates across occupation groups.

Key findings

Health Care and Social Assistance overtook Construction in 2024 as the fastest-growing source of claims, and Education and Training climbed 97.5% since 2020. At the occupation level, Teaching Professionals saw the steepest rise of any group, up 114.5% since 2020.

Tableau dashboard
Tableau dashboard · Work-related concussion claims in New Zealand, 2020–2026.
Why it matters

Concussion risk is shifting away from traditional high-risk manual trades and toward people-facing roles in healthcare and education, a useful signal for where safety investment could be redirected.

03 · Predictive analytics

SaaS churn prediction

Business question: Why do SaaS customers churn, and when in their lifecycle does the risk actually appear?

Analysis

Cleaned real-world behavioural data and built a full predictive analytics framework in Python and R, comparing XGBoost, Random Forest and a survival analysis model, then used SHAP to explain which factors were actually driving predictions rather than treating the model as a black box.

Key findings
88.3% accuracy · 87.3% F1 · 91.2% AUC-ROCThe tuned XGBoost model achieved these reported test metrics. The analysis also found satisfaction, total engagement duration and login frequency among the highest-importance predictors.
Feature importance · Random Forest and XGBoost comparison, from the research project.
Why it matters

The modelling work connects predictive performance with explainable customer behaviours, supporting more targeted retention decisions rather than relying on a churn score alone.

Tools

Python · R · XGBoost · Random Forest · SHAP
